# Pension and Annuity Decisions in Retirement

## Lump Sum vs. Annuity — The Core Decision

When a defined benefit pension plan offers a lump sum buyout option, participants face one of the most consequential financial decisions of their lives. The choice is irreversible and involves trading guaranteed lifetime income for a one-time capital payment.

**The fundamental question:** Is the pension plan's pricing of the lump sum fair relative to the annuity? And does the retiree have the discipline, health, and circumstances to manage a lump sum to produce equivalent or better lifetime income?

## Breakeven Analysis

The breakeven age is the point at which cumulative annuity payments exceed the lump sum (assuming the lump sum grows at an alternative investment rate):

**Simple breakeven:** Divide the lump sum by the annual annuity payment.
- Lump sum: $600,000
- Annual annuity: $36,000/year
- Simple breakeven: $600,000 ÷ $36,000 = **16.7 years** from retirement → age 82 if retiring at 65

**Adjusted breakeven (accounting for investment returns on lump sum):** If the lump sum earns 5% annually in a managed portfolio, the adjusted breakeven extends further. The annuity must provide income long enough to overcome the foregone investment growth.

**How to frame it for clients:** "This decision is essentially a bet on whether you will live past age 80–83. If your family has good longevity and you're in good health, the annuity is likely to win on pure math. If you have health concerns or prefer flexibility, the lump sum may be better."

## Factors Favoring Lump Sum

**1. Poor health or shortened life expectancy.** If the client has a serious health condition, family history of early mortality, or is not expected to outlive the breakeven age, a lump sum captures value regardless of longevity.

**2. Spouse has their own substantial pension or income.** If the surviving spouse will have sufficient income from their own sources, the survivor protection provided by a joint-and-survivor annuity is less critical, reducing the annuity's value.

**3. Investment competence and discipline.** Clients who have successfully managed investments and have no behavioral tendency to overspend can potentially manage a lump sum to produce equivalent or superior lifetime income.

**4. Plan insolvency concern.** For private-sector pensions, the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC) provides a backstop, but with limits. Participants in underfunded plans or plans with financially stressed sponsors may prefer a lump sum to guarantee they receive full value.

**5. Estate goals.** A lump sum can be invested and the remaining balance passed to heirs. An annuity typically ends at death (or at the end of a guarantee period). If leaving a legacy is important, a lump sum is more estate-friendly.

**6. Flexibility and control.** Large one-time expenses (healthcare, home modifications, supporting family members) are funded from a lump sum. Annuity income is fixed and cannot be accelerated.

## Factors Favoring Annuity

**1. Excellent health and longevity.** Healthy, active retirees in families with members living into their 90s will likely live past every breakeven calculation. The annuity is essentially longevity insurance.

**2. No other guaranteed income or small Social Security.** A retiree whose Social Security provides minimal income benefits greatly from another guaranteed lifetime income source to cover essential expenses.

**3. Behavioral concern about lump sum management.** Some clients will overspend, underinvest, or make poor decisions with a large lump sum. The annuity removes this risk entirely.

**4. Inflation rider available.** Some pension plans offer a cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) on the annuity. A 3% annual COLA on a pension annuity dramatically increases the annuity's long-term value and makes the breakeven analysis even more favorable to the annuity.

**5. Simplicity.** A check every month requires no investment management, no RMD calculations, no market watching. For clients who value simplicity above optimization, the annuity wins on quality-of-life grounds.

## PBGC Insurance — Private Pension Protection

The **Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation** insures private-sector defined benefit pension plans. If a plan terminates without sufficient assets to pay promised benefits, the PBGC takes over and pays benefits up to legal maximums.

**2025 maximum PBGC guarantee for single-employer plans:**
- **$6,750.68 per month** for a 65-year-old retiree (pure straight-life annuity)
- Adjusted downward for early retirement (lower cap) and upward for late retirement (higher cap)
- Joint-and-survivor and other options are adjusted from this base

**Important limitations of PBGC guarantee:**
- The guarantee applies to the *monthly benefit*, not all plan features (lump sum enrichments above minimum may not be covered)
- For very high-income executives with large pension promises, the benefit may significantly exceed the PBGC cap — only the insured portion is protected
- Multiemployer plans (union plans) have separate, lower PBGC insurance levels

**Multiemployer plan distinction:** Union and multiemployer pension plans have a separate PBGC insurance program with significantly lower guarantee limits. These plans have faced significant financial stress and several have received special government assistance under the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021. Participants in multiemployer plans should verify their plan's funded status.

## Pension Maximization Strategy

A pension maximization strategy allows a married participant to elect the **highest single-life annuity** (maximum monthly income) while using life insurance to protect the surviving spouse, rather than electing a reduced joint-and-survivor option.

**How it works:**
1. Participant elects single-life annuity (e.g., $4,500/month) instead of 100% joint-and-survivor (e.g., $3,700/month)
2. The $800/month difference ($9,600/year) is used to purchase term life insurance on the participant
3. If participant dies first, life insurance proceeds replace the pension income for the surviving spouse
4. If participant outlives the spouse, they continue receiving the higher $4,500/month

**When pension max works:**
- Participant is insurable at reasonable premium rates (health is a prerequisite)
- Term insurance premiums are significantly less than the monthly income difference
- Participant is meaningfully younger than their spouse (more years of premium payments but also more risk)

**When pension max fails:**
- Participant is uninsurable or insurance is prohibitively expensive
- Insurance lapses (premium not paid) before the participant dies — spouse receives nothing
- Participant lives significantly longer than expected and the cumulative premium cost exceeds the annuity differential
- Divorce — pension protection ends with death, not divorce

## Income Annuities — SPIAs and DIAs

Unlike variable and indexed annuities (complex investment products), pure income annuities are straightforward guaranteed-income products:

### Single Premium Immediate Annuity (SPIA)

A SPIA converts a **lump sum payment into an immediate, guaranteed income stream** for life (or for a specified period). Payments begin within one month of premium payment.

**How pricing works:** The insurance company pools longevity risk across many annuitants. Early dyers subsidize long-livers. Those who live past life expectancy receive more than they paid in; those who die early receive less.

**Payout rates (2025 approximate, vary by insurer and interest rates):**
- 65-year-old male, $100,000 SPIA, life only: approximately $590–$640/month
- 65-year-old female, $100,000 SPIA, life only: approximately $545–$595/month
- Couple (65M/62F), $100,000 SPIA, 100% joint-and-survivor: approximately $475–$520/month

**Exclusion ratio:** A portion of each SPIA payment represents return of premium (basis) and is excluded from income. The exclusion ratio = premium ÷ expected total payments over life expectancy. Once the exclusion period is complete (the retiree has received back their full premium), all subsequent payments are fully taxable.

### Deferred Income Annuity (DIA) / Longevity Insurance

A DIA converts a lump sum today into an **income stream starting at a future date** — typically age 80 or 85. Also called longevity insurance.

**Why it's powerful:** Because income begins much later, a small premium today buys surprisingly large future income. A 65-year-old spending $50,000 today might receive $1,500–$2,500/month starting at age 85. This is possible because the insurance company retains the premium for 20 years and many purchasers will not survive to age 85.

**Qualified Longevity Annuity Contract (QLAC):** A DIA purchased inside an IRA or 401(k). SECURE 2.0 increased the QLAC limit to the **lesser of $200,000 or 25% of the IRA balance** (previously $145,000). QLAC premiums are excluded from RMD calculations — reducing required distributions — until income begins. The deferral of RMDs is the primary tax advantage.

**When DIA/QLAC makes sense:** Client worried about living too long and outlasting their portfolio. Wants to "insure" against extreme longevity (living to 90+) without committing a large portion of assets to immediate annuitization. A modest QLAC premium secures late-life income while the rest of the portfolio is managed freely.

## Variable, Fixed, and Fixed Indexed Annuities

### Variable Annuities

Variable annuities invest the premium in **subaccounts** (mutual fund equivalents). Investment risk remains entirely with the policyholder. Growth can be substantial in good markets; loss can be severe in bad markets.

**Problems with variable annuities:**
- Fees are high: mortality and expense charges (1–1.5%), administrative fees, rider fees, and underlying subaccount fees typically total 2–4% annually
- Surrender charges lock the investment for 5–10 years
- Tax-deferral benefit is the primary selling point, but IRAs and 401(k)s already provide tax deferral — adding a variable annuity inside a tax-advantaged account is almost never justified (paying 2–4% in fees for a benefit already provided for free)
- Guaranteed benefit riders (GLWB, GMIB) add significant cost and complexity and are often misunderstood

**The cardinal rule:** Variable annuities inside a 401(k) or IRA are almost never appropriate. The additional fee layers provide no additional tax benefit since the account is already tax-deferred.

### Fixed Annuities

Fixed annuities guarantee a **fixed interest rate** for a specified period (similar to a CD structure). Simple, transparent, low-fee.

**Multi-Year Guaranteed Annuities (MYGAs):** Fixed annuities with a guaranteed rate for 3–10 years, then renewal at market rates. Often competitive with CDs and Treasury bonds for conservative investors who want insurance company backing and potential state guaranty association protection (covers annuities up to state limits, typically $250,000).

### Fixed Indexed Annuities (FIAs)

FIAs link credited interest to an equity index (typically S&P 500) with a **floor of 0%** (principal protection) and a **cap or participation rate** that limits upside.

**Example:** S&P 500 gains 18% in a year. FIA with 100% participation rate and 10% cap credits 10%. S&P 500 loses 15%. FIA credits 0%. Principal is preserved.

**The tradeoff:** No losses, but capped gains. Over time, the cap significantly reduces the investor's participation in equity market returns. This is a legitimate product for risk-averse investors who want market-linked growth with principal protection — but the complexity, long surrender periods, and agent commissions warrant scrutiny.

## Annuity Payout Options

When annuitizing (converting to income), common payout options:

- **Life only:** Maximum monthly income. Stops at death. No death benefit.
- **Joint-and-survivor (50%, 75%, 100%):** Continues at the stated percentage to the surviving spouse. 100% JAS provides the most survivor protection but lowest initial payment.
- **Period certain (10-year, 20-year):** If the annuitant dies before the period ends, payments continue to beneficiaries through the end of the period. After the period, life-only payments continue.
- **Cash refund / installment refund:** If the annuitant dies before receiving back the full premium, the remaining balance is paid to beneficiaries as a lump sum (cash refund) or continued payments (installment refund).

**For married couples:** Joint-and-survivor 100% provides maximum survivor protection. Single clients may prefer period certain or cash refund to protect against early-death loss of premium.
