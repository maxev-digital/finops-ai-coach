# Budgeting and Cash Flow Management

## Emergency Fund First

Before investing, build an emergency fund of 3-6 months of essential expenses in a high-yield savings account (HYSA). This prevents liquidating investments at a loss when unexpected expenses arise.

Guidelines by situation:
- Stable income, dual-earner household: 3 months
- Single income or variable pay: 6 months
- Self-employed or specialized worker: 6-12 months

As of 2024, HYSAs offer 4.5-5% APY — far above traditional bank accounts. There is no reason to keep emergency funds earning 0.01%.

## The 50/30/20 Framework

A simple starting budget allocation:
- 50% Needs: housing, utilities, groceries, transportation, minimum debt payments
- 30% Wants: dining, entertainment, subscriptions, travel
- 20% Savings and debt payoff: retirement contributions, emergency fund, extra debt payments

Adjust for your situation: high cost-of-living cities may require 60% on needs. High earners may save 30-40%.

## Zero-Based Budgeting

Every dollar gets assigned a purpose. Income minus all planned spending and saving equals zero. More disciplined than percentage frameworks — useful when aggressively paying off debt. Tools: YNAB, EveryDollar.

## High-Impact Savings Levers

In order of typical impact:
1. Housing: if over 30% of gross income, consider downsizing or adding a roommate
2. Transportation: total cost of ownership (payment + insurance + gas) often surprises people
3. Food: grocery planning and reducing dining out typically saves $200-500/month per household
4. Subscriptions: audit annually — average American spends $200+/month without realizing it

## Automating Savings

Automate transfers to savings and investment accounts on payday. What you never see in checking, you don't spend. Automation outperforms willpower every time.

## Tracking Net Worth

Net worth = Assets minus Liabilities. Track monthly. Positive trends matter more than the absolute number early in your financial journey.
