# Investing Fundamentals

DISCLAIMER: This document is general financial education and does not constitute investment advice. Consult a licensed financial advisor before making investment decisions.

## Time in Market Beats Timing the Market

Consistent long-term investing outperforms market timing for the vast majority of individuals. Missing the 10 best market days in a 20-year period can cut your returns in half.

## Asset Classes

Stocks (Equities): ownership in companies. Higher expected return over long periods, higher short-term volatility. Appropriate for goals 10+ years away.

Bonds (Fixed Income): loans to governments or corporations. Lower expected return, lower volatility. Provide stability and income. Appropriate for shorter time horizons and portfolio balance.

Cash and Cash Equivalents: savings accounts, money market funds, CDs. Preserve capital, earn modest yield. Appropriate for emergency funds and goals within 1-3 years.

## Diversification

Diversification reduces risk without proportionally reducing expected return. A well-diversified portfolio holds many securities across asset classes, geographies, and sectors. A portfolio of 2-3 low-cost index funds can achieve meaningful diversification.

## Index Funds vs Active Management

Index funds track a market index (e.g., S&P 500) and charge minimal fees (0.03-0.10% expense ratio). Decades of evidence show that most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index after fees. For most individuals, low-cost index funds are the optimal core strategy.

## Risk Tolerance and Time Horizon

Common starting allocations:
- Aggressive (20+ years to goal): 90% stocks, 10% bonds
- Moderate (10-20 years): 70% stocks, 30% bonds
- Conservative (under 10 years): 50% stocks, 50% bonds
- Very conservative (under 5 years): consider high-yield savings instead of market exposure

## Dollar-Cost Averaging

Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule (e.g., $500/month) regardless of market conditions removes the stress of timing and naturally buys more shares when prices are lower.

## Tax-Efficient Account Priority

General guidance (not tax advice — consult a CPA):
1. 401(k) to the employer match (guaranteed return)
2. HSA if eligible (triple tax advantage)
3. Roth IRA (tax-free growth)
4. 401(k) beyond the match (tax-deferred growth)
5. Taxable brokerage account (most flexible, least tax-advantaged)
