# IRS Retirement Account Rules: RMDs, Rollovers, and Distributions

Source: IRS Publication 590-B, SECURE Act (2019), SECURE 2.0 Act (2022).

---

## Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

RMDs are mandatory annual withdrawals from tax-deferred retirement accounts beginning at the required beginning date. Withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income. Applies to Traditional IRAs, SEP-IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, 457(b) government plans.

**Roth IRAs: NO RMD during owner's lifetime.** (Roth 401ks had RMDs pre-2024; SECURE 2.0 eliminated them.)

### RMD Age by Birth Year (SECURE 2.0)
| Birth Year | RMD Start Age |
|-----------|--------------|
| Before 1951 | 70½ or 72 (based on prior law) |
| 1951 – 1959 | 73 |
| 1960 and later | 75 |

**Required Beginning Date:** April 1 of the year following the year you reach RMD age. In subsequent years: December 31. Taking two distributions in year one (by April 1 and by Dec 31) can cause a large taxable income spike — often better to take the first RMD in the year you reach RMD age.

### RMD Calculation
RMD = Prior December 31 account balance ÷ IRS Uniform Lifetime Table life expectancy factor

Example: Age 75, IRA balance $600,000 on Dec 31 prior year. Life expectancy factor = 24.6.
RMD = $600,000 ÷ 24.6 = $24,390

Factors decrease each year, making RMDs a larger percentage of the account over time.

**Spouse sole beneficiary exception:** If your only beneficiary is a spouse more than 10 years younger, use the Joint and Last Survivor Table (smaller factors = smaller RMDs).

### RMD Penalty
Failure to take RMD: 25% excise tax on the shortfall (reduced to 10% if corrected within a 2-year correction window). IRS Form 5329.

### QCDs — Qualified Charitable Distributions
Age 70½ or older can direct up to $105,000 (2025, indexed) per year from a Traditional IRA directly to a qualified charity. QCD counts toward RMD but is excluded from gross income. Strategy: satisfies RMD without increasing AGI, which matters for IRMAA, SS taxation, ACA subsidies. Cannot deduct as charitable contribution if excluded from income.

## 72(t) SEPP — Substantially Equal Periodic Payments

IRS exception allowing penalty-free IRA distributions before age 59½. Must continue for the longer of 5 years or until reaching 59½. Three IRS-approved calculation methods:

1. **Required Minimum Distribution Method:** Uses IRS life expectancy table. Amount fluctuates annually. Lowest distributions.
2. **Fixed Amortization Method:** Fixed annual payment based on life expectancy and chosen interest rate. Highest distributions. Amount locked in.
3. **Fixed Annuitization Method:** Uses annuity factor from IRS mortality tables. Similar to amortization. Amount locked in.

Modifying or stopping payments before the term ends triggers retroactive 10% penalty on all prior distributions plus interest. Serious commitment — use only when genuinely needed.

## Early Distribution Penalty Exceptions (10% Penalty Waiver)

Distributions before age 59½ from IRAs and 401(k)s are subject to 10% early withdrawal penalty plus ordinary income tax, UNLESS an exception applies:

**IRA Exceptions:**
- Death or disability
- Substantially equal periodic payments (72t SEPP)
- Unreimbursed medical expenses over 7.5% of AGI
- Health insurance premiums while unemployed (receiving unemployment compensation)
- First-time home purchase (up to $10,000 lifetime)
- Qualified higher education expenses
- IRS levy
- Qualified reservist distributions
- Birth or adoption (up to $5,000 per event) — SECURE Act
- Emergency personal expense (up to $1,000/year, once per 3 years) — SECURE 2.0
- Domestic abuse survivor (up to $10,000 or 50% of vested balance) — SECURE 2.0
- Terminal illness — SECURE 2.0

**Additional 401(k) / Workplace Plan Exceptions:**
- Separation from service at age 55 or later (age 50 for public safety employees)
- Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO) — divorce
- Plan loan offset (involuntary)

## IRA Rollovers and Transfers

**Direct Rollover (trustee-to-trustee transfer):** Funds move directly between institutions. No withholding. No 60-day clock. No limit on frequency. Preferred method.

**60-Day Indirect Rollover:** Distribution paid to you. You have 60 days to deposit into a qualifying account. 20% mandatory federal withholding applies — you must make up the withheld amount from other funds to avoid it being treated as a distribution. Limit: one indirect rollover per 12-month period across all IRAs (IRS Announcement 2014-15).

**Rollover Timing Rules:**
- 401(k) → Traditional IRA: anytime upon separation or plan distribution
- Traditional IRA → Roth IRA (conversion): anytime, taxable event
- Roth IRA → Roth IRA: direct transfer only (no 60-day rollover restrictions for same-type)
- After-tax 401(k) → Roth IRA: allowed; earnings go to Traditional IRA (or pay tax)

**Net Unrealized Appreciation (NUA):** If employer stock inside 401(k) has grown significantly, NUA strategy allows distribution in-kind. Cost basis taxed as ordinary income; appreciation (NUA) taxed at long-term capital gains rates when sold. Can save significantly vs. rolling to IRA where all distributions would be ordinary income.

## Inherited IRA Rules (Post-SECURE Act 2019)

### Eligible Designated Beneficiaries (EDBs) — stretch allowed
- Surviving spouse
- Minor child of original owner (until age 21, then 10-year rule applies)
- Disabled or chronically ill individuals
- Beneficiary not more than 10 years younger than owner

EDBs may stretch distributions over their life expectancy using Single Life Table.

### Non-Eligible Designated Beneficiaries (NEDBs) — 10-year rule
Anyone else (adult children, siblings, non-spouse beneficiaries). Must empty account by December 31 of the 10th year following the year of death. No annual RMD required during years 1–9 IF the original owner died before their RMD start date. If owner died on or after RMD start date, annual RMDs required in years 1–9.

### No-Designated Beneficiary (estate, charity, certain trusts) — 5-year rule
Must empty account within 5 years of owner's death.

**Planning implication:** Large inherited IRAs hitting adult children in their peak earning years creates major tax problem. Consider converting owner's IRA to Roth before death — heirs inherit tax-free, still under 10-year rule but no tax owed on distributions.
