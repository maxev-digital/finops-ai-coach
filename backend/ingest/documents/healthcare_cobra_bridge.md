# COBRA Continuation Coverage: Bridge Between Employer Plans

## What COBRA Is

The Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1985 (COBRA) requires most employers with group health plans and 20 or more employees to offer continuation coverage to individuals and their covered dependents who lose group health coverage due to specific qualifying events.

COBRA allows you to keep your exact current employer health plan for a defined period — same doctors, same network, same coverage — rather than immediately losing coverage at job transition.

## Who Must Offer COBRA

Employers covered by federal COBRA:
- Private-sector employers with 20 or more employees (counting both full-time and part-time on a typical business day)
- Federal government employees (covered by different continuation rules)
- State and local government employees (covered by separate rules in most states)

**NOT covered by federal COBRA** (20-employee threshold not met):
- Small employers with fewer than 20 employees
- Self-employed individuals
- Churches (certain exemptions apply)

For small employer plans, many states have "mini-COBRA" laws requiring some form of continuation coverage for smaller groups — rules vary significantly by state.

## Qualifying Events and Coverage Duration

| Qualifying Event | Covered Persons | Duration |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------|
| Employee's termination (voluntary or involuntary, other than gross misconduct) | Employee, spouse, dependents | 18 months |
| Employee's hours reduction (falling below benefit-eligibility hours) | Employee, spouse, dependents | 18 months |
| Employee's gross misconduct | None — COBRA is not required | N/A |
| Employee's disability at termination/reduction | Employee, spouse, dependents | 29 months (if disabled at time of qualifying event) |
| Employee's death | Spouse, dependents | 36 months |
| Employee's divorce or legal separation | Spouse (and dependents) | 36 months |
| Employee enrolls in Medicare | Spouse, dependents | 36 months |
| Dependent child loses dependent status (age out at 26, marriage) | Former dependent | 36 months |

**Important**: The 18-month period for job loss or hours reduction can be extended to 29 months if the qualified beneficiary is determined to be disabled at the time of the qualifying event (per Social Security's definition of disability) AND notifies the plan administrator within 60 days of the disability determination.

## The Cost of COBRA: Understanding the Sticker Shock

This is the most common surprise. Under COBRA, you pay:
- **Your former contribution**: The amount employees typically pay
- **The employer's contribution**: What the employer was paying on your behalf
- **2% administrative fee**: Added on top of the full premium

Most employees never see what their employer pays for health insurance. When they leave and see the full COBRA premium, the amount is often shocking:

**Typical 2025 COBRA premiums**:
- Individual coverage: $600-$900/month
- Family coverage: $1,600-$2,200/month

If you were paying $250/month for individual coverage at your employer, the employer was likely contributing $400-$600/month. Your COBRA premium would be $650-$900/month — a dramatic increase.

This cost comparison makes COBRA vs. ACA marketplace analysis essential for most people who lose employer coverage.

## COBRA Election and Deadlines

**Election period**: You have 60 days from the later of:
- The date your coverage ended, OR
- The date you were sent your COBRA election notice

You can elect COBRA at any point within this 60-day window.

**Strategic use of the election window**: If you are healthy and unlikely to need care, you can wait the full 60 days before electing. If a health event occurs during the 60-day window, you can elect COBRA retroactively — coverage will be effective back to the date your prior coverage ended. You will owe all premiums for that retroactive period, but the claims will be covered.

**First premium payment**: After electing COBRA, you have 45 days to make the first premium payment (which covers all retroactive premiums since coverage ended). After the first payment, subsequent premiums are due monthly with a 30-day grace period.

## When COBRA Makes Financial Sense

### COBRA May Be Preferable When:

1. **Significant ongoing medical care**: You are mid-treatment for cancer, a surgery has been scheduled, or you are managing a complex condition with established specialist relationships. COBRA provides immediate continuity of care without provider changes.

2. **Deductible already partially met**: If you are 9 months into a calendar year and have already met $3,000 of a $4,000 deductible under your employer plan, switching to a new plan resets the deductible. COBRA lets you continue with the partially-met deductible for the remainder of the year.

3. **Specific medications on a favorable formulary**: If you use specialty medications that may not be on a new plan's formulary (or are on a less favorable tier), COBRA preserves your current formulary access.

4. **Limited marketplace network in your area**: In rural areas or markets with limited insurer competition, ACA marketplace plans may have narrow networks that exclude your preferred providers. COBRA preserves a broader network.

5. **Short expected coverage gap** (less than 2-3 months): If you have a new job starting in 6 weeks, COBRA may be simpler than enrolling in a marketplace plan and canceling it immediately.

### Marketplace May Be Preferable When:

1. **Eligible for ACA subsidies**: If your income qualifies for marketplace subsidies, a subsidized Silver plan may cost dramatically less than COBRA for equivalent or better coverage.

2. **Healthy and don't need continuity of care**: Lower-cost Bronze or Catastrophic plans may provide adequate protection at much lower cost than COBRA.

3. **Long gap before new employer coverage**: If you're between jobs for 6+ months, the cost savings from a subsidized marketplace plan vs. COBRA compound significantly.

## Losing a Job and COBRA vs. Marketplace

For people who lose employment and income:
- Loss of employer coverage is a qualifying life event that opens a Special Enrollment Period for ACA marketplace
- Lower income due to unemployment may qualify for Medicaid (income below 138% FPL in expansion states) or significant ACA subsidies
- Do not automatically default to COBRA without running the ACA subsidy comparison first

**Steps when losing employer coverage**:
1. Note the date employer coverage ends
2. Apply for ACA marketplace coverage (can begin 60 days before losing coverage)
3. Calculate expected income for the coverage year — reduced income may mean significant subsidies
4. Compare COBRA premium (known) vs. ACA premium after estimated subsidy
5. If ACA is cheaper and network is acceptable: enroll in ACA; do not elect COBRA
6. If specific ongoing care needs favor COBRA: elect COBRA for continuity; transition to marketplace during the next open enrollment or after completing treatment

## Mini-COBRA for Small Employers

For employees losing coverage at employers with fewer than 20 employees, federal COBRA does not apply. Many states have enacted "mini-COBRA" laws:
- Coverage: Similar continuation rights but under state law
- Duration: Varies by state (typically 6-18 months)
- Cost: Similar structure — employee pays full group premium plus admin fee

The mini-COBRA election periods and premium payment deadlines also vary by state. Check your specific state's requirements.

## COBRA and Medicare: The Critical Interaction

If you lose employer coverage and elect COBRA but are eligible for Medicare (or near Medicare eligibility), be aware:

**Electing COBRA after Medicare eligibility begins**: If you are already enrolled in Medicare when you lose employer coverage, COBRA is secondary to Medicare — it only covers gaps in Medicare coverage. COBRA is not a substitute for Medicare enrollment.

**Medicare enrollment during COBRA**: Enrolling in Medicare while on COBRA is NOT a qualifying event that ends your COBRA obligation — you remain responsible for COBRA premiums but Medicare becomes primary.

**Impact on Medicare enrollment window**: Electing COBRA does not extend your Medicare enrollment Special Enrollment Period. If you are near 65 and on COBRA, you should understand your Medicare enrollment deadlines independently of your COBRA status.
