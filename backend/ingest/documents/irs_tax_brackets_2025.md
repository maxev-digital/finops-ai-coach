**Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT):** 3.8% surtax on lesser of net investment income or MAGI exceeding $200,000 (single) / $250,000 (MFJ). Stacks on top of capital gains rates — effective top rate on investment income for high earners = 23.8%.

## Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)

AMT applies if AMT liability exceeds regular tax liability. Triggered by preference items: large ISO stock option exercises, high state taxes in high-income states, accelerated depreciation.

| | Single | MFJ |
|-|--------|-----|
| Exemption | $88,100 | $137,000 |
| Phase-out begins | $626,350 | $1,252,700 |
| AMT rate | 26% up to $239,100; 28% above | Same |

## IRMAA — Medicare Premium Surcharges

Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount. Based on MAGI from 2 years prior (2025 premiums use 2023 income). Can appeal with life-changing event (retirement, divorce, death of spouse).

## Kiddie Tax

Applies to unearned income (dividends, capital gains, interest) of children under 19 (or full-time students under 24) with at least one living parent. Unearned income above $2,700 is taxed at parent's marginal rate. Relevant for UGMA/UTMA accounts.

## Key Planning Concepts

**Marginal vs. Effective Rate**
Marginal rate = rate on the last dollar earned. Effective rate = total tax / total income. A MFJ couple earning $250,000 has a 24% marginal rate but a much lower effective rate because the first $23,850 is taxed at 10%, the next $73,100 at 12%, etc.

**Bracket Management**
With known income (salary, RMDs), you can fill remaining bracket capacity with Roth conversions, capital gain harvesting, or Roth contributions at low rates. The 12%/22% boundary ($96,950 MFJ) and 22%/24% boundary ($206,700 MFJ) are the most common planning thresholds.

**Tax-Gain Harvesting**
At 0% long-term capital gains rate, taxpayers below $96,700 (MFJ) can realize gains in taxable accounts and reset cost basis with no federal tax. Strategy: sell appreciated shares, immediately repurchase (no wash sale rule for gains — only losses). Useful in low-income years (early retirement, sabbatical, gap year).

**Roth Conversion Window**
The years between retirement (income drops) and age 73 (RMDs begin) + Medicare at 65 (IRMAA cliff) create an optimal window for Roth conversions. Convert enough to fill the 22% or 24% bracket without crossing into the next IRMAA tier.

**Marriage Penalty / Bonus**
MFJ brackets are not exactly double single brackets at all levels. High earners see a penalty (35% bracket starts at $501,051 MFJ vs. $626,350 × 2 = $1,252,700 single). Lower earners often see a bonus (two moderate incomes may combine into a lower bracket than filing separately).
