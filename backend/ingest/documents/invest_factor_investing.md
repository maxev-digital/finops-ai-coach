# Factor Investing: Systematic Return Premiums

## What Is Factor Investing

Factor investing is the systematic exposure to well-documented characteristics of securities that have historically been associated with higher risk-adjusted returns. Unlike traditional active management, which relies on manager skill and discretion, factor investing is rules-based and transparent: a set of characteristics defines the universe of investments.

The key requirements for a factor to be considered investable and credible:
1. **Persistent**: Premium documented over many decades and multiple market environments
2. **Pervasive**: Present across multiple asset classes, geographies, and time periods
3. **Robust**: Holds across multiple definitions of the factor, not just one specific construction
4. **Investable**: Can be captured in a real portfolio after costs with reasonable liquidity
5. **Intuitive**: Grounded in economic rationale — either a risk story or a behavioral story

Factors that meet these criteria represent systematic sources of return that can be accessed through low-cost, rules-based vehicles — not traditional active management.

## The Five Canonical Factors

### 1. Market Beta (Equity Risk Premium)

The most foundational factor: equities earn more than risk-free assets because investors require compensation for bearing equity risk. This is not controversial. The equity risk premium has been positive over virtually every multi-decade period in developed markets.

- **Premium**: Approximately 5-7% annually above T-bills over long periods
- **Risk story**: Equities represent claims on uncertain future cash flows; investors require higher expected returns for this uncertainty
- **Implementation**: Any broad equity index fund (VTI, VOO) captures this factor

### 2. Size Premium (Small Cap)

Small-capitalization stocks have historically outperformed large-capitalization stocks over long periods. Fama and French documented this in 1992 as part of the three-factor model.

- **Historical premium**: Approximately 2% annualized over large-cap
- **Risk story**: Small companies are less liquid, more financially fragile, and less diversified — investors require a premium for these risks
- **Behavioral story**: Small stocks are less covered by analysts and institutional investors — pricing inefficiencies exist
- **Implementation**: Vanguard Small-Cap (VB), Avantis US Small Cap (AVSC), iShares Russell 2000 (IWM)
- **Caveat**: Premium has been weaker in recent decades; most pronounced when combined with value (small-cap value)

### 3. Value Premium

Value stocks — those trading at low prices relative to fundamental measures (book value, earnings, cash flow) — have historically outperformed growth stocks (high-priced relative to fundamentals).

- **Historical premium**: Approximately 3-4% annualized over growth stocks — the most documented equity factor premium
- **Risk story**: Value stocks are often financially distressed — their cheapness reflects genuine business difficulties; investors require extra return for this distress risk
- **Behavioral story**: Investors systematically extrapolate recent growth trends too far — growth stocks become overpriced; value stocks become underpriced; mean reversion creates the premium
- **Implementation**: Vanguard Value (VTV), iShares MSCI USA Value (VLUE), Dimensional US Value (DFUSX), Avantis US Small Cap Value (AVUV)
- **Important caveat**: Value underperformed growth by a historic magnitude from approximately 2007 to 2020 (tech-driven growth dominance). The premium recovered strongly in 2021-2022. Investors must be prepared for multi-year — even decade-long — periods of underperformance relative to the market.

### 4. Profitability/Quality Premium

Profitable, financially healthy companies outperform unprofitable or financially weak companies, even after controlling for price. Robert Novy-Marx documented this in 2013; AQR subsequently built it into the quality factor.

- **Historical premium**: Approximately 3% annualized for high-profitability vs. low-profitability stocks
- **Intuition**: Highly profitable companies reinvesting efficiently produce better shareholder returns than capital-intensive, low-return businesses; the market systematically undervalues stability
- **Implementation**: iShares MSCI USA Quality (QUAL), Vanguard VFQY, Dimensional Quality funds
- **Tax efficiency**: Quality strategies tend toward lower turnover — more tax-efficient than momentum

### 5. Momentum Premium

Stocks with strong recent performance (typically defined as the past 12 months, excluding the most recent month) tend to continue outperforming over the following 3-12 months. Jegadeesh and Titman documented this in 1993; it has been replicated in every major market globally.

- **Historical premium**: Approximately 4-6% annualized — the strongest documented factor premium
- **Behavioral story**: Investors underreact to new information initially (anchoring), then overreact as the trend becomes obvious (herding) — both contribute to momentum
- **Implementation**: iShares MSCI USA Momentum (MTUM), AQR Momentum ETF
- **Key limitation**: Highest turnover of any factor — significant tax drag in taxable accounts; crash risk (momentum strategies can suffer extremely sharp reversals during market regime changes — 2009 "momentum crash" was -73% in two months)

## The Low Volatility Factor

A paradox in finance: stocks with lower volatility and lower beta have historically produced higher risk-adjusted returns than high-volatility stocks — the opposite of what the Capital Asset Pricing Model predicts.

- **Reason**: Institutional investors constrained to benchmarks cannot hold low-beta stocks at full weight; leverage-constrained investors seeking high returns bid up high-volatility stocks; the result is systematic mispricing
- **Implementation**: iShares MSCI USA Min Vol (USMV), Invesco S&P 500 Low Volatility (SPLV)
- **Characteristic**: Lower drawdowns during market crises; participation in bull markets with lag; better Sharpe ratio but lower absolute return in some periods

## Tilting vs. Pure Factor Portfolios

Most retail investors benefit from a **tilted** approach rather than a pure factor portfolio:

**Tilt approach (recommended for most)**: Hold a core broad market index fund and add a factor-overweight position. Example: 65% VTI (total market) + 35% AVUV (small-cap value). This maintains broad diversification while adding a systematic factor tilt.

**Pure factor portfolio**: Hold only factor-screened securities with no broad market core. Higher tracking error, more pronounced underperformance during factor drawdowns, requires higher behavioral tolerance.

The tilt approach provides most of the expected premium with less volatility relative to a market benchmark, making it easier to maintain during extended periods of factor underperformance.

## Factor Premiums Are Not Free Lunch

Three important limitations:

**Tracking error**: Factor tilted portfolios will diverge from broad market benchmarks during factor-unfavorable periods. Small-cap value underperformed the S&P 500 by an accumulated 60%+ from 2007 to 2020. An investor who abandoned the tilt during that period locked in the underperformance and missed the subsequent recovery.

**Crowding risk**: As factors become widely known and implemented, the premium may compress. The value premium in US large-cap has been less pronounced since the Fama-French publication made it widely known. However, the premium has not been fully arbitraged away because it comes with genuine risk (distress risk, career risk, tracking error risk) that limits how aggressively it can be exploited.

**Factor timing**: Research does not support timing factors — attempting to rotate into factors when they appear cheap and out when they appear expensive. Point-in-time factor valuations have poor predictive ability. A consistent, long-term commitment to factor exposure is the evidence-based approach.

## International Factor Premiums

The value, size, and profitability premiums are documented internationally — they are not US-specific phenomena. This provides an additional diversification argument for international factor exposure:

- The value premium in international developed markets (Europe, Japan) has been more consistent in recent decades than in US markets
- Avantis International Small Cap Value (AVDV) and Dimensional International Small Cap Value (DISVX) provide factor-tilted international exposure
- Factor diversification (US + International factors with partial correlation) smooths the factor premium experience across market cycles

## Cost Consideration

Factor ETFs cost 0.15-0.40% vs. plain index funds at 0.03-0.05%. The additional cost of approximately 0.10-0.35% per year must be justified by the expected factor premium capture. Based on historical data, documented factor premiums average 2-4% annually — the additional cost of factor exposure is small relative to the expected premium. However, the premium is uncertain in any given period; the cost is certain. This is the fundamental risk of factor investing.
