# Capital Gains Tax Planning

Source: IRS Publication 550, IRC §1221–§1231, §1411.

---

## Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains

**Short-term:** Asset held 12 months or less. Taxed as ordinary income at marginal rate (up to 37%).

**Long-term:** Asset held more than 12 months. Taxed at preferential rates: 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on taxable income. Plus 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) for higher earners.

**Effective top rate on long-term gains:** 20% + 3.8% NIIT = 23.8% for high earners.

**Holding period planning:** If you're close to the 12-month mark on a gain, waiting even one day past the anniversary can drop the tax rate dramatically — especially if you're in a high ordinary income bracket.

---

## 0% Capital Gains Rate — Tax Gain Harvesting

In 2025, taxpayers with taxable income below $48,350 (single) or $96,700 (MFJ) pay 0% on long-term capital gains and qualified dividends.

**Tax-gain harvesting:** Deliberately sell appreciated assets in a 0% year to reset cost basis. Immediately repurchase the same shares. No tax owed. Future gains are measured from the new (higher) cost basis, reducing future taxes.

There is NO wash-sale rule for gains — only for losses. You can sell and immediately repurchase.

**When this applies:**
- Early retiree before Social Security / RMDs begin
- Sabbatical or gap year with low income
- Children with investment income below the threshold (watch kiddie tax rules under age 19)
- Working taxpayer who front-loaded retirement contributions, reducing taxable income below threshold

---

## Tax-Loss Harvesting (TLH)

Sell assets at a loss to offset capital gains. Losses offset gains dollar-for-dollar; excess losses up to $3,000 can offset ordinary income per year. Additional unused losses carry forward indefinitely.

**Wash-sale rule (IRS §1091):** Cannot repurchase the "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale or the loss is disallowed and added to the new shares' cost basis. "Substantially identical" means same security or options/contracts on same security — not similar ETFs tracking the same index (e.g., VTI → ITOT is generally acceptable).

**Optimal TLH conditions:**
- High ordinary income year (losses offset income at 37% if used against ordinary income)
- Large realized gains from other sales (1:1 offset)
- Tax-deferred bonds/income in retirement accounts (no annual taxable interest)
- Equity exposure in taxable accounts only

**TLH limitations:**
- Defers taxes rather than eliminates them (new lower cost basis means more gain later)
- Administrative complexity if done frequently
- Not worth it if you plan to donate appreciated shares to charity (donate the gain directly, avoid TLH)

## Asset Location Strategy

Place assets where their tax treatment is most efficient:

| Asset Type | Optimal Account |
|-----------|----------------|
| Tax-efficient (index funds, ETFs, growth stocks held long-term) | Taxable brokerage |
| Tax-inefficient (bonds, REITs, active funds with high turnover, short-term gains) | Tax-deferred (IRA, 401k) |
| High-growth assets with expected appreciation | Roth IRA (tax-free growth) |
| International funds with foreign tax credit | Taxable (credit is lost in tax-deferred accounts) |

Asset location is distinct from asset allocation. You can hold the same overall portfolio mix (60/40) while locating bonds in IRA and stocks in Roth.

## Qualified Opportunity Zones (QOZ)

Invest capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund within 180 days of the sale. Benefits:
- Defer the original gain until December 31, 2026 (or until QOF interest is sold, if earlier)
- Hold 10+ years: appreciation on the QOF investment is tax-free

Post-2026, the original deferral expires and the gain is taxable. The 10-year tax-free growth benefit remains. Primarily useful for investors with large capital gains who believe in specific opportunity zone investments.

## Donating Appreciated Securities

Donate appreciated stock (held >12 months) directly to a charity or donor-advised fund instead of selling and donating cash:
- You deduct the full fair market value (not just cost basis)
- You never pay capital gains tax on the appreciation
- Charity receives the full value (charities are tax-exempt)

**Example:** You bought stock at $10,000 now worth $50,000. Selling and donating cash: pay tax on $40,000 gain (potentially $8,000 in tax at 20%), donate after-tax proceeds. Donating the stock directly: deduct $50,000, owe zero capital gains tax. The charity gets $50,000 instead of ~$42,000.

## Real Estate Capital Gains

**Primary residence exclusion (IRC §121):** Exclude up to $250,000 (single) / $500,000 (MFJ) of gain on sale of primary residence. Must have owned AND lived in the home 2 of the last 5 years. Partial exclusion available if you fail to meet requirements due to job change, health, or unforeseen circumstances.

**Depreciation recapture:** Rental property depreciation taken must be recaptured at 25% (unrecaptured §1250 gain) even if the rest of the gain qualifies for long-term rates.

**1031 Like-Kind Exchange:** Defer capital gains on investment real estate by exchanging into another investment property. Must identify replacement property within 45 days and close within 180 days. Defers but does not eliminate tax. "Die with it" strategy: heirs receive stepped-up basis at death, eliminating the deferred gain entirely.

**Stepped-Up Basis:** Assets held until death receive a basis equal to fair market value on date of death (or 6 months later if alternate valuation date elected). Eliminates all embedded capital gains. Key planning point: do NOT gift highly appreciated assets to heirs while alive — they inherit your low cost basis. Let them inherit through the estate for the step-up.

## State Capital Gains Taxes

Most states tax capital gains as ordinary income. States with no income tax (no capital gains tax): Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington (no income tax, but has a separate capital gains tax on gains over $250,000 at 7%), Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska.

California does not conform to federal long-term capital gains preferential rates — all gains taxed as ordinary income up to 13.3%.
