# ACA Marketplace Subsidies: Planning for Pre-Medicare Coverage

## Who Qualifies for ACA Subsidies

The Affordable Care Act provides Premium Tax Credits (PTCs) to individuals and families who purchase health insurance through the ACA marketplace (HealthCare.gov or state exchanges) and whose household income falls within certain ranges.

**Standard eligibility**: Household income from 100% to 400% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). The specific dollar amounts adjust annually based on FPL updates.

**Enhanced subsidies (Inflation Reduction Act 2022)**: The American Rescue Plan (2021) and Inflation Reduction Act (2022) expanded premium subsidies beyond the traditional 400% FPL cliff through at least 2025. Under the enhanced rules:
- Households above 400% FPL qualify for credits that cap their benchmark plan premium at 8.5% of household income
- No income ceiling for subsidy eligibility — very high incomes may still receive a small credit if the benchmark plan cost exceeds 8.5% of income

**Legislative risk**: These enhanced subsidies were scheduled to expire after 2025. Congressional action would be required to extend them. The status of these enhancements should be confirmed with current law when advising clients.

**Must also**: Not have access to affordable employer-sponsored coverage (affordable = employee-only premium not exceeding a set percentage of household income); not be enrolled in Medicaid or Medicare.

## How the Premium Tax Credit Works

The Premium Tax Credit is calculated based on the cost of the "benchmark plan" — the second-lowest-cost Silver plan available in the consumer's geographic area. The credit equals the cost of that benchmark plan minus the consumer's expected contribution (a percentage of household income).

**Example (2025, estimated)**: Family of 4 with $80,000 household income (roughly 300% FPL). Expected contribution: approximately 6-8% of income. Benchmark Silver plan costs $2,200/month. Credit = $2,200 - (6.5% × $80,000/12) = $2,200 - $433 = $1,767/month credit.

The credit can be:
- **Advance payments**: The IRS pays the credit directly to the insurer monthly, reducing your monthly premium — most common approach
- **Claimed on tax return**: Pay full premiums during the year and claim the credit on Form 8962 at tax filing

## MAGI for ACA Purposes: What Counts

ACA Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) includes most types of income:
- Wages, salaries, tips
- Self-employment income
- Rental income
- Investment income (dividends, interest, capital gains — including from mutual fund distributions)
- Business income from Schedule C, E, F
- Taxable Social Security benefits
- Tax-exempt interest (municipal bond interest is counted even though not taxable for income tax)
- Foreign income exclusions are added back
- IRA and 401(k) distributions

**NOT included in ACA MAGI**:
- **Roth IRA distributions**: Qualified Roth distributions do not count as ACA MAGI — this is a critical planning point
- Return of basis from non-deductible IRA contributions (Form 8606 basis)
- Life insurance proceeds
- Inheritances
- Non-taxable Social Security benefits (below provisional income thresholds)

## Pre-Medicare Retirement and ACA: The Planning Opportunity

Retiring before age 65 (when Medicare begins) creates a potential 3-5 year window where strategic income management can generate substantial ACA subsidies.

**The scenario**: A couple retires at age 62. Both have Traditional IRA balances but no pension. Social Security is delayed to 70. Their income is entirely within their control — they can take IRA distributions in any amount needed.

**The opportunity**: If they manage their income to stay below 400% FPL (or, under enhanced subsidies, at any level where the benchmark plan exceeds 8.5% of income), they qualify for meaningful subsidies.

**Roth withdrawals as the key tool**: Roth IRA distributions don't count as ACA MAGI. A couple who has Roth IRA assets can fund expenses from Roth withdrawals while keeping their ACA MAGI artificially low — paying minimal to no premium for marketplace coverage. This is one of the most powerful applications of having Roth savings.

**Partial Roth conversion strategy**: Instead of large Roth conversions during early retirement (which would spike ACA MAGI and eliminate subsidies), use those years to live on Roth IRA basis (contributions, always available tax-free) or take modest Traditional IRA distributions within the subsidy-eligible income range.

## Roth Conversion Interaction: The Subsidy Cliff Tradeoff

Roth conversions count as ACA MAGI dollar-for-dollar. This creates a direct conflict between two attractive pre-Medicare planning strategies:

1. **Roth conversions during low-income early retirement years** (take advantage of low brackets before RMDs begin)
2. **ACA subsidy optimization** (keep income low to maximize health insurance subsidies)

A large Roth conversion in an ACA subsidy year can:
- Push income above the enhanced subsidy threshold, dramatically increasing health insurance costs
- Eliminate cost-sharing reductions for Silver plan buyers at lower income levels
- In the extreme case (under old rules below 400% FPL), eliminate all subsidy eligibility

**Modeling approach**: Calculate the subsidy value at different income levels. Compare the marginal cost of a Roth conversion (higher taxes + lost subsidy) vs. the benefit (future tax-free distributions, reduced future RMDs). In many cases, the subsidy loss is worth $5,000-$15,000/year — this is real money that must factor into conversion calculations.

## Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR)

Cost-Sharing Reductions are an additional benefit for lower-income enrollees (100%-250% FPL) that reduce deductibles, copays, and maximum out-of-pocket exposure within a Silver plan:

| Income Level (% FPL) | Silver plan deductible (approximate) | Max OOP |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------|---------|
| 100-150% FPL | $150-$300 | $1,000-$1,500 |
| 150-200% FPL | $500-$800 | $2,500-$3,000 |
| 200-250% FPL | $1,500-$2,000 | $4,000-$5,000 |
| 250%+ FPL | Standard Silver plan terms | ~$9,450 |

**Critical rule**: CSRs are only available if you enroll in a Silver plan. Enrolling in Bronze or Gold eliminates CSR access even if you qualify income-wise. For lower-income marketplace enrollees, Silver plans with CSR are often dramatically more valuable than their listed actuarial value suggests.

## Income Estimation and Reconciliation

At enrollment, you estimate your income for the coming year. The IRS reconciles the advance premium tax credits against actual income on your tax return (Form 8962):

**If actual income was higher than estimated** (you received more advance credits than you were entitled to): You must repay the excess. The repayment is capped for those under 400% FPL:
- Repayment cap for 100-200% FPL: $375 (single) / $750 (family)
- Repayment cap for 200-300% FPL: $950 (single) / $1,900 (family)
- Repayment cap for 300-400% FPL: $1,600 (single) / $3,200 (family)
- Above 400% FPL: Must repay the full excess — no cap

**If actual income was lower than estimated**: You receive the additional credit as a refund on your tax return.

**Planning implication**: For those above 400% FPL where there is no repayment cap, income estimation accuracy is critical. An unexpected capital gain event (sale of property, mutual fund distribution) can result in full repayment of advance credits claimed — a significant unexpected tax bill.

## Special Enrollment Periods

Outside of the annual Open Enrollment Period (November 1 through January 15 in most states), you can only enroll in marketplace coverage after qualifying life events:
- Loss of other health coverage (job loss, COBRA ending, end of Medicaid eligibility)
- Marriage or domestic partnership
- Birth, adoption, or placement of a foster child
- Change in primary place of residence to a new coverage area
- Gaining citizenship
- Income change that makes you newly eligible for subsidies (projected income change)
- Retirement that causes loss of employer coverage

Most SEPs provide a 60-day window from the qualifying event to enroll.
