# IRS Contribution Limits 2025

Source: IRS Rev. Proc. 2024-40, IR-2024-285. All limits are for tax year 2025.

---

## Workplace Retirement Plans

**401(k), 403(b), 457(b), Thrift Savings Plan**
- Employee elective deferral: $23,500
- Catch-up contribution (age 50–59, 64+): $7,500 → total $31,000
- SECURE 2.0 enhanced catch-up (age 60–63): $11,250 → total $34,750
- Annual additions limit (employee + employer): $70,000
- Compensation limit used in calculations: $350,000

**SIMPLE IRA / SIMPLE 401(k)**
- Employee contribution: $16,500
- Catch-up (age 50+): $3,500 → total $20,000
- SECURE 2.0 enhanced catch-up (age 60–63): $5,250 → total $21,750

## Individual Retirement Accounts

**Traditional IRA and Roth IRA (combined limit)**
- Contribution limit: $7,000
- Catch-up (age 50+): $1,000 → total $8,000

**Roth IRA Income Phase-Out (ability to contribute directly)**
- Single / Head of Household: $150,000 – $165,000 MAGI
- Married Filing Jointly: $236,000 – $246,000 MAGI
- Married Filing Separately (lived with spouse): $0 – $10,000 MAGI
- Above phase-out: use Backdoor Roth (contribute non-deductible Traditional IRA → convert)

**Traditional IRA Deductibility Phase-Out (if covered by workplace plan)**
- Single: $79,000 – $89,000 MAGI
- Married Filing Jointly (covered spouse): $126,000 – $146,000 MAGI
- Married Filing Jointly (non-covered spouse, other spouse has plan): $236,000 – $246,000 MAGI
- Not covered by any workplace plan: fully deductible at any income

## Self-Employed Retirement Plans

**SEP-IRA**
- Maximum contribution: lesser of 25% of net self-employment compensation OR $70,000
- Net SE compensation = Schedule C net profit minus half of SE tax
- No catch-up contribution for SEP-IRA
- Deadline: tax filing deadline including extensions (Oct 15 for sole props)

**Solo 401(k) (One-Participant 401k)**
- Employee elective deferral: $23,500 (same limits as regular 401k, including catch-ups)
- Employer profit-sharing: up to 25% of W-2 wages (or ~20% of SE net income for sole props)
- Total combined limit: $70,000 (or $77,500 / $81,250 with catch-up)
- Spousal coverage: if spouse earns income from the business, both can contribute
- Roth Solo 401k option: available if plan documents allow

**SIMPLE IRA (for businesses with ≤100 employees)**
- See workplace plan limits above
- Employer must contribute: either 2% non-elective for all eligible OR 3% matching

## Health Savings Account (HSA)

Requires enrollment in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). Triple tax advantage: deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free qualified medical withdrawals.

- Self-only HDHP coverage: $4,300
- Family HDHP coverage: $8,550
- Catch-up (age 55+): $1,000 additional per eligible individual
- HDHP minimum deductible (self-only): $1,650; (family): $3,300
- HDHP out-of-pocket maximum (self-only): $8,300; (family): $16,600
- Contribution deadline: tax filing deadline (April 15, no extensions)

HSA after age 65: non-medical withdrawals taxed as ordinary income (no 20% penalty). Effectively becomes a second IRA.

## Other Tax-Advantaged Accounts

**Health FSA (Flexible Spending Account)**
- Employee contribution limit: $3,300
- Dependent Care FSA: $5,000 per household ($2,500 MFS)

**529 Education Savings Plan**
- No annual federal limit, but annual gift tax exclusion applies: $19,000/year per beneficiary without gift tax return
- Superfunding: 5-year election allows lump-sum contribution of up to $95,000 (single) / $190,000 (MFJ) per beneficiary without gift tax, treated as $19,000/year over 5 years
- No income limits on contributions
- SECURE 2.0: unused 529 funds (after 15 years) can roll to Roth IRA for beneficiary, lifetime limit $35,000, subject to annual Roth IRA contribution limits

**Coverdell ESA**
- Contribution limit: $2,000 per beneficiary per year
- Income phase-out: Single $95,000–$110,000; MFJ $190,000–$220,000
- Funds must be used by age 30 (or rolled to another family member's Coverdell)

## Social Security Wage Base

- OASDI taxable wage base: $176,100
- Employee SS tax rate: 6.2% (employer matches)
- Employee Medicare tax rate: 1.45% (no wage base cap)
- Additional Medicare Tax: 0.9% on wages over $200,000 (single) / $250,000 (MFJ) — employer does NOT match this

## Key Planning Notes

1. **Max the sequence:** Emergency fund → 401k match capture → HSA max → IRA max → 401k max → taxable brokerage
2. **Catch-up priority:** Workers age 60–63 have the highest catch-up limits ever (SECURE 2.0). A 62-year-old can defer $34,750 in a 401k alone.
3. **Backdoor Roth:** High earners above Roth phase-out can still get Roth money in. Contribute non-deductible Traditional IRA → immediately convert. Watch for pro-rata rule if other Traditional IRA balances exist.
4. **Mega Backdoor Roth:** If your 401k plan allows after-tax contributions and in-service withdrawals, you can add up to ~$46,500 in after-tax funds → convert to Roth. Plan-specific — check your Summary Plan Description.
5. **Deadline differences:** 401k contributions must come from payroll by Dec 31. IRA contributions can be made until April 15. SEP-IRA until Oct 15 (with extension).
