# Debt Management Strategies

## Good Debt vs Bad Debt

Not all debt is equal. A low-rate mortgage on an appreciating asset is different from 24% APR credit card debt.

Rule of thumb: aggressively pay off any debt above 7-8% interest. Below that, investing may mathematically outperform early payoff over long time horizons. High-interest consumer debt is a guaranteed negative return on your net worth.

## Debt Snowball

Pay minimums on all debts. Put every extra dollar toward the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate.

Pros: psychological wins build momentum, works well for people who have struggled with consistency.
Cons: may pay more total interest than the avalanche method.

## Debt Avalanche

Pay minimums on all debts. Put every extra dollar toward the highest interest rate first.

Pros: mathematically optimal, minimizes total interest paid over time.
Cons: if the high-rate debt is large, early progress feels slow.

Best for: analytically motivated people who can stay consistent without quick wins.

## Credit Card Strategy

If carrying a balance: stop adding new charges, apply for a 0% balance transfer card if your credit score qualifies, then attack the balance systematically.

If paying in full monthly: credit cards are a useful tool for 1-5% cash back or travel rewards on spending you would make anyway. Set autopay to the full statement balance — one missed payment erases months of rewards.

## Student Loan Considerations

Federal loan options worth reviewing: Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) caps payments at a percentage of discretionary income. Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) forgives remaining balances after 10 years of payments in qualifying public or nonprofit roles.

Refinancing into private loans eliminates federal protections — only do this after exhausting federal repayment options and with a clear payoff plan.

## Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

DTI = Monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Below 36%: healthy. 36-49%: concerning, limit new debt. 50%+: high stress risk, aggressive paydown recommended. Mortgage lenders typically require DTI below 43%.
