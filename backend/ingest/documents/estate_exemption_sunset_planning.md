# Estate Tax Exemption Sunset Planning: 2025 Urgency

## The Sunset Provision

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 (TCJA) dramatically increased the federal estate and gift tax exemption — approximately doubling it from its pre-TCJA level. This increase was not made permanent. The TCJA contained a sunset provision: these higher exemption amounts expire on December 31, 2025, reverting to pre-TCJA levels (adjusted for inflation).

**Current 2025 exemption**: $13,990,000 per individual ($27,980,000 for a married couple)

**Projected post-sunset exemption (2026)**: Approximately $6,800,000-$7,200,000 per individual (the 2017 baseline of $5,490,000 adjusted for inflation using CPI through 2026). The exact amount depends on when legislation is enacted and the inflation adjustment calculation.

**Top estate tax rate**: 40% on assets above the exemption

## Who Is Affected

The sunset primarily affects:
- **Individuals with net worth between ~$7M and ~$14M**: Currently fully protected by the 2025 exemption; after sunset, exposed to estate tax on the excess above ~$7M
- **Married couples with combined assets $14M-$28M**: Currently using portability to protect both spouses' exemptions; after sunset, exposed to estate tax on the amount above ~$14M combined
- **Business owners with illiquid business interests**: Business value often drives estates into this range; the owner may not have liquid assets to pay estate tax without forcing a sale of the business
- **Individuals with real estate-heavy estates**: Real estate values have increased substantially; coastal property owners may have estate values in this range

**Not affected**: Individuals with estates clearly below $7M (protected after sunset) or clearly above $14M (already doing estate tax planning at current levels).

## No Clawback: Use It Without Risk

A critical planning point: The IRS finalized regulations in November 2019 confirming there will be no "clawback" of gifts made under the higher exemption if the exemption later decreases. If you use $13,990,000 of exemption making gifts in 2025, and the exemption drops to $7M in 2026, you will NOT owe gift/estate tax on the gifts made at the higher exemption level.

The IRS's "no clawback" rule means the 2025 exemption can be used with confidence. The phrase used in estate planning: "use it or lose it" — the opportunity to use the higher exemption disappears after December 31, 2025, but gifts already made are permanently sheltered.

## The Urgency

To capture the benefit of the 2025 exemption level, irrevocable gifts or irrevocable trust funding must be completed by December 31, 2025. This means:

- Trust documents must be drafted, reviewed, and signed
- Assets must actually be transferred (titled, deeded, retitled) to the trust before year-end
- Appraisals for closely held business interests, real estate, or other illiquid assets must be completed in time to support the gift
- Legal and accounting team coordination required

The practical planning timeline for year-end 2025:
- **September-October**: Finalize strategy, engage estate attorney, begin asset appraisals
- **October-November**: Draft and review trust documents, identify assets to fund
- **November-December**: Execute documents, transfer assets, file or plan Form 709 gift tax returns for year 2025

Waiting until December creates significant risk of not completing in time.

## Strategies to Lock In the Higher Exemption

### Spousal Lifetime Access Trust (SLAT)

The most popular 2025 planning strategy for married couples. Grantor spouse funds an irrevocable trust for the benefit of the other spouse and descendants. Each spouse can fund a SLAT for the other (but see reciprocal trust doctrine cautions in the irrevocable trusts document).

- Uses the grantor's lifetime exemption
- Removes assets from the taxable estate
- Beneficiary spouse retains access for health, education, maintenance, and support
- Relatively accessible trust structure — does not require business interests or complex assets

### Gift to Children's Trust (ILIT, Dynasty Trust)

Direct gift of assets to an irrevocable trust for children and descendants. No retained access by the grantor or spouse.
- Maximum estate tax removal
- Maximum asset protection benefit
- But: grantor loses all access to the gifted assets

### Grantor Retained Annuity Trust (GRAT)

Fund a GRAT with appreciated or rapidly appreciating assets to pass appreciation above the 7520 rate to heirs. While a GRAT itself is not a pure "use of exemption" strategy (a zeroed-out GRAT uses no exemption), it is effective for transferring future appreciation. Often combined with outright gifts to use exemption.

### Family Limited Partnership Formation and Gift

Form an FLP or family LLC, contribute business assets, real estate, or investment assets, then gift limited partnership interests to trusts for heirs. Valuation discounts reduce the effective value of the gift, allowing more assets to be transferred using the same amount of exemption.

Example: $10M in assets transferred to FLP; gifts of FLP interests valued at $7M-$8M due to discounts; only $7M-$8M of exemption used to remove $10M of assets from the estate.

### Accelerating Charitable Gifts

For charitably inclined individuals, large gifts to DAFs or CRTs before year-end can reduce the taxable estate while achieving charitable goals. These do not use the estate/gift exemption — charitable deductions reduce the taxable gift/estate directly.

## Monitoring for Congressional Action

As of mid-2025, there is significant legislative uncertainty about whether:
1. TCJA provisions will be fully extended (exemption stays at current levels permanently)
2. TCJA provisions will be partially extended (some increase, not full doubling)
3. TCJA provisions will be allowed to expire (revert to ~$7M)
4. New legislation could change rates or exemptions differently from the baseline TCJA sunset

The key principle: complete any planning that makes sense regardless of whether the exemption sunsets. If the exemption is extended, the planning is still valid and beneficial. If it sunsets, the planning captures the higher exemption. The asymmetry favors acting before year-end for large estates.

## State Estate Tax Interaction

Approximately 12 states and the District of Columbia have their own estate tax with exemptions lower than the federal level:
- Massachusetts and Oregon: $1M exemption
- New York: ~$7.16M exemption (2025)
- Illinois: $4M exemption
- Washington state: ~$2.2M exemption

For residents of these states, estate tax planning may already be appropriate for smaller estates — the sunset primarily affects the federal calculation. A married couple in Massachusetts with $5M combined might already need planning for state estate tax regardless of the federal sunset.

Portability (the ability to use a deceased spouse's unused federal exemption) does NOT apply to most state estate taxes — each state must be evaluated separately.

## Professional Team Required

The 2025 sunset creates a planning window that genuinely requires a coordinated professional team:
- **Estate attorney**: Drafts and reviews all trust documents; advises on strategy suitability
- **CPA**: Projects income tax implications of asset transfers; coordinates gift tax return preparation (Form 709)
- **Financial planner**: Models the overall financial impact; determines which assets to fund into trusts vs. retain; ensures the plan is integrated with the client's liquidity needs

This is not a DIY area. The consequences of improper trust funding, stale appraisals, or missed deadlines can be irreversible. Start the process early and allow sufficient time for the professional team to work effectively.
