# Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Insurance: Standardized Gap Coverage

## What Medigap Covers

Medicare Supplement insurance (Medigap) fills the cost-sharing "gaps" in Original Medicare (Parts A and B). The federal government standardizes Medigap plan designs, identified by letters (A through N). Every insurer selling Plan G must offer the same benefits as every other insurer selling Plan G — only the premium differs.

**Common coverage gaps that Medigap fills**:
- Part A hospital coinsurance and costs up to an additional 365 days after Medicare benefits end
- Part A deductible ($1,676 in 2025) — covered by Plans B, D, G, K, L, M, N
- Part B deductible ($257 in 2025) — covered ONLY by Plan C and Plan F (both unavailable to new Medicare enrollees since January 1, 2020)
- Part B coinsurance or copayment (20% of Medicare-approved amount) — covered by most plans
- Skilled nursing facility coinsurance — covered by Plans C, D, F, G, M, N
- Foreign travel emergency coverage — covered by Plans C, D, F, G, M, N (80% up to plan limits)

## The Two Most Popular Plans: G and N

### Plan G (Most Comprehensive Available to New Enrollees)

Since Plans C and F are no longer available to new Medicare enrollees (those who became eligible on or after January 1, 2020), Plan G has become the most comprehensive option available.

**Plan G covers**:
- Part A coinsurance and hospital costs
- Part A deductible
- Part A hospice care coinsurance/copayments
- Part B coinsurance (20%) — all of it
- Skilled nursing facility coinsurance
- Foreign travel emergency (80% up to $50,000 lifetime)

**Plan G does NOT cover**:
- Part B deductible ($257/year in 2025) — the only out-of-pocket cost for Plan G enrollees

**After paying the Part B deductible once per year**, Plan G essentially covers all Medicare-approved costs with no further out-of-pocket exposure — regardless of how much healthcare you use.

**Who Plan G is for**: People who use healthcare regularly, want predictable costs, or have chronic conditions requiring frequent medical care. The higher premium vs. Plan N is justified when healthcare utilization is significant.

### Plan N

Similar to Plan G but with two additional cost-sharing elements:
- **$20 copay for office visits** (doctor visits) that are otherwise covered 100% by Medicare
- **$50 copay for emergency room visits** not resulting in hospital admission
- Does cover the Part A deductible (like Plan G)
- Does NOT cover the Part B deductible (like Plan G) AND requires excess charges payment

**Part B excess charges**: Providers who do not "accept assignment" (do not accept Medicare's approved amount as payment in full) can charge up to 15% more than the Medicare-approved amount. Plan N does NOT cover these excess charges; Plan G does. In most states, this is minimal exposure because nearly all providers accept assignment. But in states with more non-participating providers (certain urban areas), Plan N's excess charge exposure matters.

**Who Plan N is for**: Healthy Medicare enrollees who rarely visit doctors but want catastrophic protection. The lower premium compared to Plan G may offset the copays for low-utilization enrollees.

### Plan K and Plan L

Higher cost-sharing plans with lower premiums. Plan K covers 50% of several components; Plan L covers 75%. Come with out-of-pocket maximums ($7,220 for K and $3,610 for L in 2025). Much less popular. Appropriate only for very healthy enrollees primarily seeking catastrophic protection at minimum premium.

## The Open Enrollment Window: Guaranteed Issue Period

**Guaranteed issue during Medigap Open Enrollment**: You have a 6-month window starting the first month you are age 65 OR OLDER AND enrolled in Part B. During this window:
- No insurer can deny you Medigap coverage
- No insurer can charge you more due to pre-existing conditions or health history
- No insurer can impose waiting periods for pre-existing conditions

**After the open enrollment window**: Insurers in most states can:
- Deny your application based on health history
- Charge you more than a healthy applicant ("medical underwriting")
- Impose waiting periods for coverage of pre-existing conditions

This makes the Medigap open enrollment window one of the most consequential and time-limited financial planning decisions. Missing it — or choosing Medicare Advantage during the window and then wanting to switch to Medigap later — can result in denied coverage or dramatically higher premiums.

**States with guaranteed issue protections**: About 10 states (including New York and Massachusetts) have continuous guaranteed issue — insurers must offer Medigap to anyone enrolled in Medicare, anytime. These states have somewhat different rules from the federal framework.

## Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap: The Strategic Choice

This is the most important healthcare decision at Medicare eligibility. The financial implications span decades.

### Medicare Advantage (Part C)

**Advantages**:
- Often $0 or very low monthly premium
- Includes dental, vision, hearing, fitness memberships, and other extras not in Original Medicare
- Usually includes Part D drug coverage
- Annual out-of-pocket maximum provides catastrophic protection
- Simple: one plan for all coverage

**Disadvantages**:
- HMO networks: must use in-network providers; specialist referrals required
- PPO networks: out-of-network care covered but at higher cost
- Prior authorization: many procedures, specialist referrals, medications, and inpatient stays require insurer approval before service
- Variable and unpredictable out-of-pocket costs: copays for each service accumulate; heavy users can hit the out-of-pocket maximum ($9,350 for in-network in 2025, higher for out-of-network)
- Plan changes annually: your plan's network, formulary, premiums, and benefits can change each year — requires annual re-evaluation
- Switching back to Medigap requires medical underwriting in most states

### Medicare Supplement (Medigap) + Part D

**Advantages**:
- Any provider who accepts Medicare, anywhere in the US — no network
- No referrals, no prior authorization (beyond any Original Medicare requirements)
- Predictable costs: pay the monthly premium and the Part B deductible ($257/year) — minimal additional out-of-pocket
- Stable benefits: Medigap plans are standardized; your benefits don't change year-to-year (though premiums may increase)
- Freedom for complex conditions: see specialists at major academic medical centers nationwide

**Disadvantages**:
- Higher predictable monthly premium ($100-$300/month for Plan G, depending on age and location)
- Separate Part D plan purchase required (additional $20-$60/month typically)
- No additional benefits (dental, vision, hearing must be purchased separately or managed out-of-pocket)

### Who Should Choose Which

**Medigap + Original Medicare is better for**:
- People with complex, chronic conditions needing frequent specialist care
- People who travel extensively or split time between states
- People who want certainty about costs and maximum provider choice
- People in areas where Medicare Advantage networks are narrow or poorly rated
- People who have established care with providers who may not be in Advantage networks

**Medicare Advantage is better for**:
- Healthy Medicare enrollees who rarely use healthcare
- People whose primary care and specialist network is within a high-rated Advantage HMO
- People who highly value the added dental/vision/hearing benefits
- People in areas with highly-rated (4-5 star) Advantage plans and broad networks
- People with limited budgets where the lower premium is the deciding factor

## Premium Variation and Shopping

All Plan G policies in a geographic area offer identical benefits. The premiums, however, vary significantly:

**Pricing methods**:
- **Community-rated (no-age-rated)**: All enrollees in the plan pay the same premium regardless of age. Premiums rise with inflation but not with your age. Most favorable long-term for younger enrollees.
- **Issue-age rated**: Premium is based on your age when you buy the policy; does not increase simply because you age. Premiums are higher for older buyers at issue but remain stable.
- **Attained-age rated**: Premium increases as you age. Most common nationally. Cheapest at enrollment but escalates over time — can become very expensive in later decades.

When comparing Medigap premiums, identify the pricing method and project costs at age 75, 80, and 85 — not just the current rate. The cheapest Plan G at 65 may become the most expensive at 80 under attained-age pricing.

## Medigap Switching Risk

Once you leave the Medigap open enrollment window, switching plans or insurers requires medical underwriting in most states. If your health has deteriorated since original enrollment, you may be:
- Denied coverage by a new insurer
- Offered coverage only at a significantly higher premium
- Required to accept waiting periods for pre-existing conditions

The risk of switching from Medicare Advantage back to Medigap is particularly significant: Advantage enrollees who develop serious health conditions may find they cannot obtain Medigap coverage at affordable rates — or at all (outside of states with continuous guaranteed issue). This effectively locks some enrollees in Advantage plans at a vulnerable time.

This lock-in risk is a material consideration when choosing between Advantage and Medigap at initial enrollment.
