# Maximizing Employer Benefits

## Health Savings Account (HSA) — Triple Tax Advantage

An HSA offers three tax benefits simultaneously: contributions are pre-tax, growth is tax-free, and qualified medical withdrawals are tax-free. Available only with a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP).

2024 limits: $4,150 individual / $8,300 family. Age 55+ can contribute an extra $1,000. Funds roll over year to year — no "use it or lose it."

After age 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed like a Traditional IRA, making the HSA effectively a stealth retirement account.

Strategy: If you can afford medical expenses out of pocket, invest your HSA contributions for long-term growth. Save receipts — you can reimburse yourself years later, tax-free.

## 401(k) Match Optimization

Always contribute at least enough to capture the full employer match — it's the highest guaranteed return available. Beyond the match, consider whether to prioritize the Roth 401(k) option (if available) based on your current vs. expected future tax rate.

## ESPP — Employee Stock Purchase Plan

ESPPs let employees buy company stock at a discount (typically 10-15%) using after-tax payroll deductions. A 15% discount equals a guaranteed 17.6% gain at the moment of purchase.

Key risk: concentration. Holding large amounts of a single employer's stock amplifies both upside and downside. Most financial planners recommend selling ESPP shares promptly to capture the discount and diversify.

## FSA vs HSA

FSA (Flexible Spending Account): pre-tax contributions for medical or dependent care, but subject to "use it or lose it" rules (some plans allow a $610 rollover or grace period). Best for predictable recurring expenses. HSA requires an HDHP but offers superior long-term flexibility.

## Open Enrollment Checklist

1. Model health plan options based on expected usage — compare premium savings vs. deductible risk
2. Max HSA/FSA contributions if you have a qualifying plan
3. Review and update beneficiary designations
4. Consider voluntary benefits: accident, critical illness, legal plans
5. Check life and disability insurance coverage against your dependents' needs
