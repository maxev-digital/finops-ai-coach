# Tax-Loss Harvesting

Source: IRS Publication 550, IRC §1211, §1212, §1091.

---

## Core Concept

Tax-loss harvesting (TLH) is the practice of selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains and reduce taxable income. The goal is to capture the tax benefit of losses today while maintaining similar market exposure.

**Losses offset gains dollar-for-dollar.** Excess losses over gains can deduct up to $3,000 against ordinary income per year. Remaining unused losses carry forward indefinitely to future years.

---

## The Tax Value of a Harvested Loss

The value depends on the rate at which losses are used:

| Used against | 2025 rate | Value per $1,000 harvested |
|--------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Short-term capital gains | 10–37% marginal rate | $100–$370 |
| Long-term capital gains | 0–23.8% | $0–$238 |
| Ordinary income ($3,000 limit) | 10–37% marginal rate | $100–$370 |
| Carried forward (future gains) | Depends on future rate | Present value discounted |

**Most valuable scenario:** High-income taxpayer (37% bracket) with short-term gains. Each $1,000 harvested saves $370 in federal tax (plus state).

**Least valuable scenario:** Low-income taxpayer in 0% capital gains bracket. Loss harvesting against zero-rate gains saves nothing.

## The Wash-Sale Rule (IRC §1091)

**The 30-day window:** You cannot sell a security at a loss and repurchase a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before OR after the sale. Total window: 61 days (30 before, sale date, 30 after).

If you violate the wash-sale rule:
- The loss is disallowed
- The disallowed loss is added to the cost basis of the repurchased shares (deferred, not eliminated)
- The holding period of the original shares carries over

**"Substantially identical"** securities:
- Same stock or bond ✓ (cannot repurchase)
- Options and contracts on the same stock ✓
- Same mutual fund ✓
- Different share classes of the same fund ✓ (e.g., VTSAX → VTSMX are identical)

**NOT substantially identical (generally acceptable swaps):**
- Similar ETF or fund tracking a different index (VTI → SCHB; both are total market but different indexes)
- Different company in same sector (selling Apple, buying Microsoft)
- Different bond maturity or issuer
- Going from ETF to mutual fund if they track different indexes

Caution: The IRS has not provided bright-line rules for ETF swaps. Tax professionals generally accept fund-to-different-fund-different-index as acceptable. Document your reasoning.

**Wash sales in IRAs:** If you sell a security at a loss in a taxable account and repurchase the same security in an IRA or Roth IRA within 30 days, the wash-sale rule still applies and the loss is permanently disallowed (you cannot recover the basis in the IRA).

## When to Harvest Losses

**Natural triggers:**
- Market corrections and drawdowns (the most common opportunity)
- Sector rotation (one holding is down while another is up)
- Portfolio rebalancing (you're selling an overweight asset that is down)
- Concentrated position reduction combined with TLH in another holding

**Year-end harvesting:**
Many advisors do an annual TLH sweep in October–November to book losses before year end. December harvesting is possible but creates timing risk.

**Real-time automated TLH (robo-advisor approach):**
Betterment, Wealthfront, and other automated platforms harvest losses continuously at the lot level, sometimes daily. Advantage: never miss an opportunity. Disadvantage: potential wash-sale issues if you hold the same securities in other accounts (IRAs, employer 401k).

---

## Practical Limitations and Risks

**Basis reset:** Harvesting resets cost basis to the lower sale price. Future gains are larger, creating a future tax liability. TLH defers taxes — it does not eliminate them (unless you donate the securities or hold until death for stepped-up basis).

**Transaction costs:** Commissions, bid/ask spreads, and funds with redemption fees eat into the benefit. With commission-free trading at most brokers, transaction costs are minimal for liquid ETFs.

**Complexity at tax time:** Multiple harvesting events, substitute securities, and wash-sale tracking across accounts require careful record-keeping. 1099-B reports may not correctly account for wash sales across multiple accounts.

**Diminishing returns:** After years of heavy harvesting, your portfolio may have few remaining losses. The first years of TLH in a new portfolio typically generate the most value.

---

## TLH Across Multiple Accounts — Coordination Required

The wash-sale rule applies across all accounts, including IRAs and 401(k)s. Example failure mode:
- Sell VTI at a loss in taxable account (intending to harvest)
- Your Betterment Roth IRA automatically reinvests dividends into VTI within 30 days
- Wash-sale triggered — loss disallowed permanently (can't recover in IRA)

**Best practice:** Coordinate securities holdings across all accounts. Use substitute securities that are not held in any other accounts (IRAs, 401k, spouse's accounts) when harvesting.

---

## Interaction with Capital Gains Rate

At 0% long-term capital gains rate (taxable income below $48,350 single / $96,700 MFJ in 2025), harvesting losses against long-term gains produces zero benefit. In this case:
- Consider tax-GAIN harvesting instead (realize gains at 0% to reset basis higher)
- Save loss harvesting for higher-income years

**Optimal strategy:** Harvest losses in high-income working years at 37% ordinary income rate. Take gains in low-income retirement years at 0% long-term rate.

---

## State Tax Considerations

Most states allow capital loss deductions, generally mirroring federal treatment. California, for example, allows the same $3,000 ordinary income offset. At California's top 13.3% rate, each $1,000 of harvested losses saves an additional $133 in state taxes on top of federal savings.

Some states have their own wash-sale rules; others do not conform to federal. Check state-specific treatment for large harvesting events.
