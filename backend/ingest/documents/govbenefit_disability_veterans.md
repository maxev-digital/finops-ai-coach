# Social Security Disability, SSI, and Veterans Benefits

## Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI): How It Works

Social Security Disability Insurance is a federal program that pays monthly benefits to workers who become disabled and are unable to engage in substantial gainful activity (SGA). SSDI is an earned benefit — you must have a sufficient work history to qualify.

### Eligibility Requirements

**Work credits**: SSDI requires a minimum number of Social Security work credits earned before the onset of disability:
- Generally need 40 credits (10 years of work), with 20 of those earned in the 10 years before disability
- Younger workers need fewer credits (a 26-year-old needs only 14 credits for SSDI eligibility)

**The disability definition** (very strict): The SSA defines disability as the inability to engage in any substantial gainful activity (SGA) due to a medically determinable physical or mental impairment that:
- Has lasted or is expected to last for at least 12 consecutive months, OR
- Is expected to result in death

**Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) threshold in 2025**:
- Non-blind: $1,550/month in gross earnings
- Blind: $2,590/month in gross earnings

If you earn above the SGA threshold, you are presumptively not disabled for SSDI purposes regardless of your impairment.

### The Five-Step Sequential Evaluation

The SSA uses a five-step process to determine disability:

1. **Are you currently doing SGA?** If yes, not disabled. If no, continue.
2. **Is your condition severe?** Must significantly limit basic work activities. Most conditions meet this test.
3. **Does your condition meet or medically equal a listed impairment?** The SSA "Blue Book" lists specific conditions with defined severity criteria — meeting a listing is automatic approval.
4. **Can you do your past work?** If yes, not disabled.
5. **Can you do any other work?** Considers age, education, skills, and impairment to determine if you can do ANY job in the national economy.

### Application Process and Denial Rates

**Initial application**: Approximately 35% of initial applications are approved. 65% are denied.

**Reconsideration**: The first administrative appeal. Approximately 15-18% of reconsiderations are approved; 82-85% are denied again.

**Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) hearing**: Second level of appeal. Approval rate at ALJ hearing is approximately 45-55% of those who reach this stage. This is often the most important step — the hearing allows the applicant to present testimony, provide medical evidence, and have a representative advocate on their behalf.

**Appeals Council and Federal Court**: Further appeals possible but very difficult; rare approval.

**Timeline**: Average time from initial application to ALJ hearing decision: 18-36 months. Backlog at ALJ level has historically been severe.

**Representation**: Attorneys and non-attorney representatives work on contingency — fee is 25% of back pay, capped at $7,200 (2024 cap; adjusts periodically). No upfront cost; fee comes from the awarded back pay. Represented applicants have significantly higher approval rates.

### SSDI Benefit Amount

The SSDI benefit is calculated the same way as the Social Security retirement benefit — based on your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) derived from your lifetime earnings record. However, the disability benefit uses a special formula that "freezes" your earnings record at the time of disability, giving credit for earnings up to disability onset.

**Average SSDI benefit in 2025**: Approximately $1,500-$1,800/month for the average disabled worker.

For a high earner who becomes disabled at 45 with a strong earnings history, the benefit may be $2,500-$3,500/month. For someone with low lifetime earnings, it may be $800-$1,000/month.

### Medicare After SSDI Approval

SSDI recipients become eligible for Medicare after a **24-month waiting period** from the month they first receive SSDI benefits. During this waiting period, SSDI recipients need other health insurance:
- COBRA (up to 18 months, though 29 months for disabled individuals)
- Medicaid (if income and assets are low enough)
- ACA marketplace plans (SSDI recipients can use marketplace during waiting period; SSDI income counts as ACA MAGI)

After the 24-month waiting period, Medicare enrollment is automatic — same Medicare Parts A, B, C, D framework as age-65 Medicare, with the same costs and coverage.

## Supplemental Security Income (SSI)

SSI is a different program from SSDI — needs-based rather than work-history based.

**Eligibility requirements**:
- Disabled, blind, OR aged 65+ (not just disabled)
- Limited income and assets
- Must be a US citizen or meet certain immigration status requirements
- Asset limit: $2,000 for an individual, $3,000 for a couple (certain assets excluded: one home, one vehicle, retirement accounts in some cases)
- Income limits: complex; earned income, unearned income, and in-kind support are treated differently

**SSI benefit in 2025**: Federal base rate is $967/month for an individual, $1,450 for an eligible couple. Some states supplement the federal SSI amount.

**SSI vs. SSDI**: A person can receive both SSI and SSDI simultaneously (called "concurrent benefits") if their SSDI benefit is below SSI income limits. This is common for workers with limited work history who receive a very small SSDI benefit.

**Medicaid**: SSI recipients automatically receive Medicaid in most states — they do not face the 24-month Medicare waiting period.

## Veterans Disability Compensation

Veterans who suffered a service-connected injury, disease, or disability during active military service may receive monthly tax-free compensation from the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA).

### Service Connection

Disability compensation requires establishing that your current condition is "service-connected":
- The condition occurred during military service, OR
- A pre-existing condition was aggravated by military service, OR
- The condition is caused by another service-connected condition (secondary service connection)

### Disability Ratings

VA disability ratings range from 0% to 100% in 10% increments. The rating reflects the severity of the disability and determines the compensation amount.

**2025 VA disability compensation rates (approximate)**:
| Rating | Monthly compensation (veteran alone) |
|--------|--------------------------------------|
| 10% | $175 |
| 20% | $346 |
| 30% | $536 |
| 40% | $773 |
| 50% | $1,102 |
| 60% | $1,395 |
| 70% | $1,759 |
| 80% | $2,044 |
| 90% | $2,297 |
| 100% | $3,737 |

Additional compensation available for dependents (spouse, children).

**Combined ratings**: Veterans with multiple service-connected disabilities receive a "combined" rating using the "whole person" method — not simply adding percentages. Example: 50% + 30% = 65% combined, not 80%.

**100% P&T (Permanent and Total)**: The highest rating; indicates the disability is both total (prevents work) and permanent (will not improve). 100% P&T veterans receive the highest compensation and may be eligible for additional benefits (CHAMPVA for family healthcare, dependency and indemnity compensation for survivors).

### VA Healthcare

VA healthcare is separate from Medicare. Many veterans are eligible for VA healthcare based on service era, service-connected disability, income, or other criteria. Veterans can use both VA healthcare and Medicare — they are not mutually exclusive.

Veterans with service-connected disabilities generally receive priority access to VA healthcare for conditions related to their service.

### VA Pension (Not Service-Connected)

Veterans with low income and limited assets who served during wartime may be eligible for VA Pension — a needs-based benefit not related to service-connected disability:
- Must have served at least 90 days of active duty with at least 1 day during a wartime period
- Permanently and totally disabled OR age 65+
- Income and asset limits apply

### Survivors Benefits: Social Security and Veterans

**Social Security survivor benefits**: When an SSDI beneficiary dies, their survivors (spouse and children under 18 or disabled) may be eligible for survivor benefits:
- Spouse at FRA: 100% of worker's SSDI benefit
- Disabled widow(er) age 50+: 71.5-100% depending on age at claim
- Child under 18 (or 19 if full-time student): 75% of worker's SSDI benefit
- Total family maximum: 150-180% of worker's benefit, distributed among survivors

**VA Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)**: Tax-free monthly compensation for eligible survivors of veterans who died from service-connected conditions or who were rated 100% P&T for 10+ years. The 2025 basic DIC rate is approximately $1,612/month for a surviving spouse, with additions for dependent children.

**Interaction**: VA DIC and Social Security survivor benefits can be received simultaneously — they are separate programs with no offset between them.
