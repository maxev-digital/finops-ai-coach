# Federal Gift and Estate Tax 2025

Source: IRS Rev. Proc. 2024-40, IRC §2010, §2503, §2505.

---

## Federal Estate Tax

The estate tax applies to the taxable estate of a deceased U.S. citizen or resident. The taxable estate = gross estate minus allowable deductions (debts, marital deduction, charitable deduction).

**Federal Estate Tax Rate:** 40% flat rate on taxable estate above the exemption.

### Unified Credit / Basic Exclusion Amount

| Year | Per-Person Exemption | Married Couple (with portability) |
|------|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| 2025 | $13,990,000 | $27,980,000 |
| 2026 (projected if no legislation) | ~$7,000,000 | ~$14,000,000 |

**CRITICAL: The 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act doubled the exemption temporarily. These elevated amounts are scheduled to sunset December 31, 2025.** Unless Congress acts, the exemption reverts to pre-2018 levels adjusted for inflation (~$7M per individual) on January 1, 2026. Planning strategies to lock in the higher exemption before the sunset are active areas of work for estates between $7M and $14M.

### Portability
A surviving spouse can "port" the deceased spouse's unused exemption (DSUE) by filing an estate tax return within 5 years of death (even if no estate tax is owed). Portability allows the surviving spouse to effectively use both exemptions. Requires timely Form 706 filing.

## Annual Gift Tax Exclusion

Each donor can give up to $19,000 per recipient per year (2025) without filing a gift tax return (Form 709) or using any lifetime exemption. This is indexed for inflation in $1,000 increments.

- Married couples can combine: $38,000 per recipient per year via gift splitting
- Recipient's identity doesn't matter — can give to anyone (children, friends, strangers)
- Must be a completed gift of a present interest (not a future interest)
- Direct payments to educational institutions (tuition only, not room/board) and directly to medical providers are unlimited — not subject to the annual exclusion limit and don't use lifetime exemption

### 529 Superfunding
Five-year election: contribute up to $95,000 (single) / $190,000 (MFJ) per beneficiary into a 529 plan and elect to treat it as five equal annual gifts of $19,000. No gift tax return required if you stay within the limits. Donor cannot make additional exclusion gifts to the same beneficiary during the 5-year period. Locks in the money for education free from estate.

## Lifetime Gift Tax Exemption

The same $13,990,000 unified credit applies to gifts made during life. Taxable gifts (above annual exclusion) reduce the remaining estate tax exemption dollar-for-dollar. You don't pay gift tax until you've exceeded the $13,990,000 lifetime exemption; above that, 40% rate applies.

**Use-it-before-sunset strategy:** Gifts made using the higher 2025 exemption are grandfathered even if the exemption later decreases. The IRS issued final regulations (T.D. 9884) confirming no "clawback" — if you gift $13.99M in 2025 and the exemption drops to $7M in 2026, the IRS will not tax the additional $6.99M. This creates urgency for estates over $7M.

## Common Gifting Strategies

**Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT)**
Place a life insurance policy in an irrevocable trust. Death benefit proceeds are excluded from your taxable estate. Trustee pays premiums using annual exclusion gifts from you to trust beneficiaries. Proceeds pass to heirs income-tax free and estate-tax free.

**Grantor Retained Annuity Trust (GRAT)**
Transfer assets to an irrevocable trust; receive fixed annuity payments back for a term. Any appreciation above the IRS §7520 hurdle rate (published monthly) passes to heirs gift-tax free. Popular in low interest rate environments where almost any growth beats the hurdle. Zero-out GRAT: set annuity payments so remainder = $0 at IRS rates; if assets grow faster, heirs receive the excess.

**Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT)**
Transfer appreciated assets to trust. Trust sells assets tax-free (avoids immediate capital gains). You receive income stream for life or term. Remainder to charity at death. Get a partial charitable deduction upfront. Good for low-basis appreciated assets you would otherwise hold forever.

**Family Limited Partnership (FLP) / Family LLC**
Transfer family business or investment assets to FLP. Gift limited partnership interests to heirs. Interests may qualify for valuation discounts (lack of control, lack of marketability) of 20–40%. Effectively transfers more wealth using less exemption. IRS scrutinizes these arrangements heavily — requires legitimate business purpose and arms-length dealings.

**Spousal Lifetime Access Trust (SLAT)**
Irrevocable trust for the benefit of a spouse. Removes assets from your taxable estate while spouse maintains access. Allows use of elevated 2025 exemption before sunset. Risk: if marriage ends (divorce or spouse dies), you lose access to those assets.

## State Estate Taxes

12 states + Washington D.C. have a separate state estate tax, often with a lower exemption than the federal threshold. States with estate tax include Massachusetts ($2M exemption), Oregon ($1M exemption), Washington, Minnesota, Illinois, Maryland, Vermont, Connecticut, Hawaii, Maine, Rhode Island, and New York. States without estate or inheritance tax include Texas, Florida, California (no estate tax but community property rules apply).

**Inheritance tax** (different from estate tax — paid by recipient, not estate): Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Nebraska, New Jersey, Pennsylvania. Rates and exemptions vary.

## Key Deadlines

- Federal estate tax return (Form 706): 9 months after date of death (6-month extension available)
- Portability election deadline: within 5 years of date of death (extended by Rev. Proc. 2022-32)
- Annual gift exclusion: calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31)
- Gift tax return (Form 709): April 15 of the year following the gift (October 15 with extension)
